# `lens/deflections/` — deflection angles, numpy CPU

Per-call cost of `MassProfile.deflections_yx_2d_from` on the pure-numpy CPU
path, one row per mass profile. The headline question is:

> *"What does a single deflection-angle evaluation cost for this mass profile,
> and how much of that is the profile's own math versus the wrapper around it?"*

Deflection angles are the innermost lensing calculation: every ray-trace, every
mapper build, every tracer call runs through them, and a nested-sampling run
executes them millions of times. When a mass profile costs half a second per
call — as four of the nine here do — that cost is the fit.

This section is the **measurement of record** for the `numpy-deflections-cpu` epic
(PyAutoArray#514, phase 2 PyAutoGalaxy#596, phase 3 PyAutoGalaxy#598). The table below
now carries the **after-phase-3** numbers (a web-container host, `v2026.8.29.1`; the
phase-2 laptop artifacts are kept beside them); the before baseline, every phase's pass
and the full before → after analysis are in
[`results/notes/numpy_deflections_cpu.md`](../../../results/notes/numpy_deflections_cpu.md).
The `dark` and `stellar` pins were **re-pinned in phase 2** — the MGE-routed profiles
compute a corrected answer — and `stellar` again in phase 3 for one on-axis sample that
the rotation-matrix transform makes exactly zero (was 4.8e-17); each reason is recorded
as `pin_provenance`. `total` has never been re-pinned.

## The cells

Three thin files, split by mass-profile family. Each is a prologue, a pinned
`EXPECTED` block and one call into `_driver.py`:

| Cell | Profiles |
|------|----------|
| `total.py` | `Isothermal`, `IsothermalSph`, `PowerLaw`, `PowerLawSph` |
| `dark.py` | `NFW`, `NFWSph`, `gNFW`, `gNFWSph` |
| `stellar.py` | `Gaussian`, `Gaussian_sph_case` |

`autogalaxy.mp` has **no** `GaussianSph`. The spherical case is the elliptical
`Gaussian` with `ell_comps=(0.0, 0.0)`, recorded as `Gaussian_sph_case` so both
rows stay distinguishable in the dashboard.

Shared material lives beside the cells: `_driver.py` (grid construction, timing
loop, cProfile pass, pin machinery, artifact write) and `_profiles.py` (the
registry of profile class + fiducial parameters + family).

## What is measured

Three timings per profile, each a call into the **installed library** — never a
transcription of its internals:

| Timing | Call | Grid |
|--------|------|------|
| `grid2d_s` | `profile.deflections_yx_2d_from(grid=...)` | `dataset.grids.pixelization` — a `Grid2D` at sub-size 1, one point per masked image pixel |
| `irregular_s` | `profile.deflections_yx_2d_from(grid=...)` | `dataset.grids.lp.over_sampled` — the `Grid2DIrregular` carrying the radial-bin over-sampling |
| `tracer_s` | `al.Tracer([Galaxy(z=0.5, mass=profile), Galaxy(z=1.0)]).traced_grid_2d_list_from(grid=...)` | `dataset.grids.pixelization` |

Each is one warm-up call followed by the **median** of `--n-repeats` (default
20) timed calls. `n_points` for both grids is recorded alongside, so a timing is
always divisible by the work it did.

The grid construction mirrors
[`scripts/imaging/likelihood_breakdown/pixelization_numba.py`](../../misc/likelihood_breakdown/README.md)
exactly — circular 3.5" mask, `over_sample_size_via_radial_bins_from` with
`sub_size_list=[4, 2, 1]` at `radial_list=[0.3, 0.6]`,
`over_sample_size_pixelization=1` — so a number here is directly comparable to
the ray-trace step of that cell's breakdown.

`tracer_over_raw` records `tracer_s / grid2d_s`. It now lands at ≈ 0.9–1.5× for
every profile: the tracer evaluates the same deflection and pays grid bookkeeping
and plane assembly on top. Before phase 1 the **spherical** profiles came out far
*below* 1 — that inverted ratio was the symptom of finding 2 below, and it is the
thing to watch if it ever returns.

### cProfile: attribution, not timing

A **separate** pass per profile cProfiles the `Grid2D` call over
`--cprofile-repeats` (default 5) calls and stores `cprofile_top`: the top 12
functions by cumulative time, per call, as
`{func, ncalls, tottime_s, cumtime_s}`.

Those numbers are **inflated by the profiler's own tracing overhead** and are
recorded purely to say *which* function the time is charged to. Every JSON
carries a `cprofile_note` repeating that. The headline is always `grid2d_s` /
`irregular_s` / `tracer_s`, measured with the profiler off.

## Pins: the numbers must not move

A faster deflection that returns a different answer is not a faster deflection.
Each run pins three things per profile and checks them at **rtol 1e-6**:

- `abs_sum` — `sum(|alpha|)` over the full `Grid2D` deflection field, skipping
  non-finite entries
- `abs_max` — `max(|alpha|)` over the same field, likewise
- `n_non_finite` — how many entries of that field are **not** finite. Pinned in
  its own right because at least one profile has some: `PowerLawSph` returns a
  deflection field with non-finite points on the hst grid. A plain `sum` would
  collapse that profile's whole pin to `NaN`, hiding every other value behind
  one bad point; splitting the count out keeps both facts and makes a change in
  either one visible
- `sample` — the `(y, x)` deflection at **16 fixed arcsec coordinates**
  (eight on r = 1.0" at 45° steps, four on r = 0.15" inside the over-sampled
  core, four on r = 2.8" in the outer mask), evaluated on a dedicated
  `Grid2DIrregular`. Stored literally in `_driver.PIN_COORDINATES`.

Drift is **recorded, never adjudicated** — the boundary rule in
[`results/notes/design_lock_in.md`](../../../results/notes/design_lock_in.md).
A moved value prints a loud warning, lands in the JSON's `pinned_drift`, marks
the row **DRIFT** in the table above, and does **not** fail the run: the timings
are still data, the flag just says they are not comparable to the baseline.

### `--repin` policy

Pins are per-instrument and live inside each cell, between the
`BEGIN EXPECTED` / `END EXPECTED` sentinels. Do not hand-edit them.

- **First run for an instrument** — no pins exist, so the cell fills its own
  `EXPECTED` block from that run and says so loudly. Nothing was verified; the
  row shows `new`.
- **`--repin`** re-measures and rewrites the block deliberately. It:
  - **requires** `--repin-reason "<why>"`;
  - prints the full old → new diff before writing;
  - **refuses** the re-pin if any pinned value moves by more than
    `--repin-max-shift` (default `1e-3`) relative, unless `--repin-force` is
    also given — a move that large is a changed computation, not a
    re-measurement, and wants an explanation first. A refusal leaves the pins
    untouched and exits non-zero;
  - records `pin_provenance: {reason, date, max_shift}` in the result JSON;
  - runs `ruff format` over the rewritten cell so the block stays
    format-canonical for the CI gate.

Re-pin when the library **intentionally** changes what these profiles compute
(a corrected formula, a changed default). Never re-pin to silence a warning you
have not explained.

## Running

From the repo root, with threading pinned so the numbers are comparable:

```bash
# hst (default) and euclid
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/lens/deflections/total.py
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/lens/deflections/dark.py --instrument euclid

# fewer repeats while iterating
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/lens/deflections/stellar.py --n-repeats 5

# deliberate re-pin after an intended library change
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/lens/deflections/dark.py \
    --repin --repin-reason "PyAutoArray#514: vectorised gNFW quadrature"
```

`--instrument` accepts `hst` (0.05"/px) and `euclid` (0.1"/px); the dataset is
auto-simulated if missing. The remaining shared flags (`--output-dir`,
`--config-name`) behave as they do everywhere else in the repo.

## Reading the results

The spread is the point, and it splits into two separate stories.

**1. `gNFW` / `gNFWSph` are genuinely expensive math.** Both cost hundreds of
milliseconds on *either* grid. `cprofile_top` charges the time to
`mge.py:deflections_2d_via_mge_from` → `zeta_from` → the hand-rolled Faddeeva
kernel `mge.py:wofz` — the generalised NFW has no closed-form deflection and is
evaluated through a multi-Gaussian expansion. That cost is real vectorised work
and follows the profile wherever it is called from.

**2. Every `*Sph` profile *used to be* ~500× slower through the `Grid2D` entry point
than through `Grid2DIrregular` — and the difference was not the profile's math.**
Fixed in phase 1; kept here because it is what the pins and the `Grid2D` column exist
to guard. Before the fix, `IsothermalSph` cost ~0.65 s on `dataset.grids.pixelization`
and ~0.8 ms on `dataset.grids.lp.over_sampled`, despite the second grid being
*larger*. The
`cprofile_top` rows put essentially all of it under
`to_grid.py:via_grid_2d` → `builtins.getattr` → `uniform_2d.py:over_sampled` →
`over_sample_util.py:grid_2d_slim_over_sampled_via_mask_from`, and that last one
is a **per-pixel Python loop** — `linspace` twice per masked pixel, `meshgrid`
once. The `Grid2D` branch of the `@to_grid` decorator rebuilds a `Grid2D` from
the result and reads `getattr(result, "over_sampled", None)` off it; the
spherical profiles return an already-decorated `Grid2D` from their inner call,
so that attribute access triggers the lazy build. The elliptical profiles return
a raw ndarray, the `getattr` yields `None`, and nothing is built. The loop, not
the lensing math, is the whole of the cost.

The third timing is what stopped this being over-read. `Tracer` received the same
`Grid2D` and was *fast* (~2.3 ms for `IsothermalSph`) — because
`tracer_util.traced_grid_2d_list_from` converts the grid to a `Grid2DIrregular`
before calling `deflections_yx_2d_from`. **Production ray-tracing therefore never
paid this**; the penalty sat on the direct `Grid2D` API call, which is what a user
script, a plotter or a convergence/potential helper reaches for.

That distinction — expensive math versus an expensive wrapper on one entry point —
is what these cells exist to make visible. Phase 1 removed the wrapper (PyAutoArray
`92bb9b2c`, PyAutoGalaxy `2605c924`, PyAutoLens `b1515e5d3`): the `*Sph` `Grid2D`
column fell by 142×–710× and the `Tracer` column by ~2–4× across every profile, with
every pin holding at rtol 1e-6.

Phase 2 then went after finding 1 itself (PyAutoGalaxy `09785e32`, issue #596):
`scipy.special.wofz` replaces the hand-rolled Faddeeva kernel on the numpy path, and
the spherical MGE members take an exact q → 1 radial form instead of an elliptical
evaluation clamped at `q = 0.9999`. `gNFWSph` fell ~40–67× and `Gaussian_sph_case`
~4–9× (the Faddeeva call is gone entirely for them); `gNFW` ~2.5–3× and `Gaussian`
~1.5–1.8× (same call, faster and more accurate). Those four profiles' pins moved and
were re-pinned with provenance — the new values are the more accurate ones on both
mechanisms, adjudicated against mpmath at dps 40 and against the lifted clamp
respectively. `gNFW` remains the residual target: an elliptical MGE deflection needs
the Faddeeva function, so anything beyond this wants a shorter expansion or a
precision decision.
